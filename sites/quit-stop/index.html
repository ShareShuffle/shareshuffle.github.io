Still calling? Block & report +

Take it from here. You can use these steps without sending an opt-out first.

  1. Save the evidence

    Screenshot your recent calls before deleting anything. Keep any messages or voicemails, and note when you asked them to stop.

    For your report, have the caller’s number, any callback number, the dates and times, and your own number ready. If they called 30 times, say how often and over what period.

  2. Block on your iPhone

    Open Phone → Recents. Open the caller’s details; in Classic view, tap the ⓘ next to the call. Scroll down, tap Block Caller (or Block this Caller), and confirm if asked.

    For a text: open the conversation in Messages, tap the contact at the top, then find Block Caller.

    Blocked callers can still leave voicemail without a notification. A different number needs its own block.

    See Apple’s blocking guide ↗

  3. Make an official report

    In the U.S.: open the FCC form below. Under Phone Issues, choose unwanted calls/texts, then all other unwanted calls/messages. Describe what happened, add the dates and numbers, and review your report before submitting.

    Open the official FCC form ↗

    You submit on the FCC’s website. The Quit Stop doesn’t submit or store your report. Complaints help enforcement; the FCC doesn’t resolve individual unwanted-call complaints.

    For an unwanted sales call or robocall with no money lost, you can also report to the FTC at DoNotCall.gov. Lost money to a phone scam? Use ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

A few things to know +

Opt-out requests can apply to unwanted calls, too. Use a company’s text-enabled contact or designated opt-out number; a number on caller ID may not accept requests or belong to the caller.

You review and send each request in Messages. We never save the number or send anything in the background. This doesn’t block numbers or guarantee calls will stop.

Your right to opt out. The FCC recognizes STOP and QUIT, among other words, as valid ways to revoke consent when replying to an incoming text. Either can be used; sending both is not required. The rule covers certain automated calls and texts, not every unwanted call. Read the FCC rule (PDF, paragraphs 25–26).

The broader requirement to apply an opt-out across unrelated calls and texts is delayed until January 31, 2027. A text opt-out is not a blanket guarantee that all calls will stop. Read the FCC’s 2026 update (PDF).
